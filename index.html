About

Hi, I’m Eliot Wong-Toi, a PhD Candidate in Statistics at the University of California, Irvine, advised by Stephan Mandt. I enjoy working at the intersection of statistical theory, machine learning, and applied data problems. My research centers on uncertainty quantification in regression and generative models, covering mean-variance regression, generative uncertainty, and conformal prediction. My work has been supported by a Hasso Plattner Institute Research Fellowship.

At UCI, I have applied machine learning to diverse domains. I worked on sports analytics using Bayesian nonparametrics and contributed to clinical trial design software through open-source development. I also applied Bayesian modeling to radiomic features for skin cancer classification, developing predictive models from high-dimensional medical imaging datasets. During an internship at Dexcom, I developed representation learning models for time-series clustering to analyze high-frequency continuous glucose monitoring data.

I received my BS in Mathematics–Computer Science (cum laude) from UC San Diego, where I worked in the Pulmonary Imaging Lab under Rui Carlos Pereira de Sá. There, I applied deep learning methods to medical imaging, developing tools to accelerate annotation and segmentation of imaging datasets.

I will complete my PhD in December 2025 and am currently seeking roles as an Applied Scientist or Data Scientist where I can combine methodological innovation with impactful applications.
