About

Hi, I’m Eliot Wong-Toi — a PhD Candidate in Statistics at the University of California, Irvine, advised by Stephan Mandt. My research focuses on uncertainty quantification, with projects spanning heteroskedastic regression, conformal prediction, and generative models. In particular, my dissertation work develops methods for mean–variance regression and investigates how to define and apply generative uncertainty in diffusion and flow-matching models.

I received my BS in Mathematics–Computer Science (cum laude) from the University of California, San Diego, where I worked in the Pulmonary Imaging Lab under Rui Carlos Pereira de Sá. There, I applied deep learning methods to medical imaging, helping develop tools to accelerate annotation and analysis.

Beyond methodological research, I am deeply interested in applying machine learning to complex, high-dimensional data. During my internship at Dexcom, I developed representation learning models for time-series clustering on high-frequency medical data. I have also worked on sports analytics with Bayesian nonparametrics and contributed to clinical trial design tools through open-source software development.

My work has been supported by a Hasso Plattner Institute Research Fellowship. I enjoy working at the intersection of statistical theory, machine learning, and real-world data problems — bridging rigorous methods with practical applications.

I will complete my PhD in December 2025 and am currently seeking Applied Scientist or Data Scientist roles where I can continue to combine methodological innovation with impactful applications.
